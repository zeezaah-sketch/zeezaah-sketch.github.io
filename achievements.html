1. Retention improvement for a seller SaaS app

PT Gudang Ada, Sr. Product Analyst | Jakarta, July 2022 - April 2023

Context

I worked with the seller team at an e-commerce marketplace. The main product was a SaaS app that helped sellers manage inventory, track orders by status, generate invoices, and view sales and stock reports.

Problem

Retention dropped sharply after the first 30 days. Sellers installed the app, used it a few times, and then abandoned it. Monthly retention data showed that most users did not return the following month, and fewer still used the core features. The app was not giving enough value early enough to justify another visit.

Approach

  • Cohorts and segmentation. I grouped users by first-use date, then compared retention by region, acquisition channel, age, and gender. I also checked which of the four key features each cohort used in its first week.
  • Funnel analysis. I mapped account creation, first use of key features, and return usage. The drop-off was concentrated before the personalisation feature, which was the feature most likely to create value early.
  • Behaviour analysis. Users who moved through the app in sequence, inventory first, then order tracking, then invoice printing, retained much better. Users who never saw that sequence churned. Younger users explored on their own, while older users needed guidance and were more likely to leave.

Recommendations

  • Replace feature discovery by trial and error with a guided onboarding flow that walks new sellers through the highest-impact features in their first sessions.
  • Track the events that mark progress through that sequence, so onboarding can be measured and improved.
  • Prioritise guidance for older sellers, who were more likely to churn without it.

How the work was presented

I compiled the cohort analysis, funnel metrics, and behaviour findings into a report for the product and developer teams. The report connected early feature engagement to 30-day retention and proposed the specific onboarding changes and event tracking needed to test them.

Impact

  • The product team implemented a guided onboarding flow based on the recommendation.
  • 30-day retention improved after the change.
  • The work set up a way to measure onboarding, and gave marketing a clearer case for new-user campaigns because the app now delivered value earlier.

2. Workforce attendance and performance monitoring in loan collection

PT Amaan, Sr. Business Intelligence Analyst | Jakarta, Sept 2023 - June 2024

Context

I partnered with operations leadership at a fintech lender to improve workforce efficiency across field and remote teams responsible for debt recovery and borrower instalment collections.

Problem

The organisation could not reliably verify daily field presence or real-time agent activity. Without transparent visibility into on-the-ground execution, management could not validate attendance. The blind spot contributed to recovery delays, unchecked compliance violations, and operational drag.

Approach

  • Data integration and real-time tracking. I designed automated ingestion that connected mobile application check-ins with portfolio and route allocations, creating a live baseline of scheduled borrower engagements against verified on-site visits.
  • Rule engine and alerting. I configured parameterised rule sets to detect non-compliance immediately, flagging missed check-ins, delayed route starts, and abnormal stationary intervals beyond defined thresholds. Severe lapses surfaced without manual audits.
  • Exception management. I built automated exception dashboards and targeted alerts for supervisors. The system classified workforce activity into performance and delinquency tiers, so supervisors could triage the highest-risk portfolios first.

Insights

  • Automating administrative triage removes the manual reporting bottleneck and lets supervisors spend their time on high-risk portfolios and severe non-compliance.
  • Structured intervention protocols, from tiered notices to route reassignment, set clear expectations across distributed teams.
  • Timestamp and location telemetry separates systemic problems, such as routing inefficiencies or connectivity dead zones, from individual agent negligence.

Impact

  • Operational leadership could intervene on attendance lapses within minutes rather than days.
  • Collection yields improved and non-performing loan balances were reduced, supporting the link between disciplined field execution and recovery performance.
  • Fragmented, retroactive auditing was replaced with continuous, scalable performance monitoring.
